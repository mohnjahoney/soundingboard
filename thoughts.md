Think carefully about the level of detail
Part of what we're doing is both gathering and making use of mediator in a way that is hopefully new and helpful
We need to be careful about how we show this meta-data - We wanted to feel intuitive and empowering and definitely not overwhelming

Some examples:
We keep timestamps for recordings and it's possible that someone might want to know the date or even perhaps the time that the recording was made. But including your month day, our minute second information on every reference to every recording is a lot of information that will 99% of the time Not be Helpful.
Some alternatives to think about just brainstorming
When people think about the date or time of a recording I think they often want to know about the order. That is they don't care exactly what time they just want to know if it was before or after something else.
So we could offer a nice way to sort by time order. Maybe this would include filtering so we are not faced with all recordings in the library.
This might be somewhat difficult to navigate just by scrolling so perhaps we could have something like a mini map on the side with some major markers for years and months and days

It's occurring to me right now that maybe a lot of these problems have been solved in apps that organize photos.
For example in the iPhone photos app, you can create folders which are like projects and add photos to these folders you can create folders based on tags. There are ways of creating movies out of a set of photos in an automated and fairly pleasing way the way we searched through photos by year month week, etc., and the way that the representations of the photos are handled visually is pretty nice
You could imagine trying to steal the structure of that code and just swap out the inner to handle audio
We could also imagine just using it for ideas and design inspiration
It also makes an interesting point of comparison- That is presumably these are products that are designed to function quite well in their sphere, which is photos and movies. That feels like it presents an opportunity to understand more clearly what the unique needs or opportunities are for a system that deals with audio.

Regarding evaluation and scoring and judgment
We've been building in some ideas about scoring where you might use a slider to rank something or you might swipe left or right to pick recordings and I wanted to balance that kind of thinking with another perspective. And that is as much as we are looking for things that we like among things that we don't like or things that fit among things that don't fit we are also here to use this as a tool for learning. And arguably, there's as much or more learning when we fail. So the complementary perspective is instead of swiping left and having those things disappear or turning the slider score way down low and having those get cut by the threshold, another perspective is, let's really dig in deep there and take advantage of that learning opportunity and while we may not keep the audio with us or we may we want to effectively capture that lesson

This is bringing up another related idea which is more about exploration and comparison and learning. For example, let's say that we're comparing two different guitars. We make a recording of more or less the same thing with each guitar. Now we have two recordings that we can listen to back to back and think about and learn from. And it may not be about better or worse or fit versus not it may be more about deepening our understanding of our musical palette. This might serve as more of a reference or tool kit. I'm not quite finding the right word sketchbook comes to mind, but that's also not quite right anyhow lots to think about here.

The moral of this brief story is, let's think about the different roles that these recordings might play in our overall process. We might be fine-tuning, we might be exploring, we might be comparing, we might just be fucking around.

He mentions the idea of "idea maps" as opposed to just linking things
These idea maps are like "hubs"
So if you had notes A, B, C, D, .. Z, you might connect most vowels to other vowels and most consonants to other consonants and this would be a graphical mess
OR you could create a "types of letters" note and maybe a note for consonants and one for vowels
In other words, when you have fairly high connectivity, rather than spend a lot of time adding all of the many pair-wise connections, just put the list of nodes in a group (essentially saying these are all connected)
Said another way, it sometimes makes more sense to define a *class* than a *pairwise relationship*.
